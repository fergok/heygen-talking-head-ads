# AI voiceover story

A first-person story told by an ElevenLabs voiceover, such as "the gift I bought him" or "the founder's story", cut to fast, literal B-roll of real people using the product. It's longer and more of a story than `AI voiceover BOF`. The user's winning examples are in `Reference Ads/`: Stocking Stuffer V3, Family Feud V2, Knockoff V2 and Founder Red Light V2. The competitor LumiLight stocking-gifts ad is the model for the first brief (gift for him) and uses the same editing style.

Learned on 2026-11-05 from those five ads and the Founder ad's CapCut project (`1014 (1)-copy`). Treat these notes as a draft until the first ad passes review.

**The main rulebook comes first and wins:** `.claude/skills/ve-video-edit-general/references/video-editing-standards.md`. Read it in full before every edit. Sections 7 and 11a, on captions and B-roll timing, apply to this style as written. Captions and timing come from the one engine, `tools/captions/house_captions.py` (rulebook 7.0). `tools/capcut/story_ad.py` uses it and runs its checks before rendering. v1 of GIFT4HIM skipped this and failed review on every caption rule (user, 2026-11-05):
- Captions were single long lines. They must be short phrases of up to two lines, split at commas and pauses (7.6, 7.8a).
- Captions blinked off and back on. There must be no blanks: each caption holds until the next one starts (7.7).
- A caption arrived a beat after its shot changed. Caption and B-roll change on the same frame, taken from the audio onset (7.8aa, 7.8b).
- The white-box captions didn't look native. Use the house caption look from 11a (CapCut system font, white text with black stroke and soft shadow, size 69, baseline y 1465).
- Scene changes felt jittery. Keep clips 1.5–3s, entered on motion (6.7, 6.8), with no clip used twice (6.13).

## Division of work

- **Deliver a CapCut project** as well as the review-app render, so the user can tweak it or drop in B-roll. Snapshot it at hand-off with `tools/capcut/capcut_draft.py snapshot`. If the user re-cuts the voiceover in CapCut, read their voiceover pieces into `vo_pieces` in the edit and rebuild as a new version, so captions and B-roll snap to the new cut (done for GIFT4HIM v3). When the user edits it, `diff` the snapshot against their edited project, and turn each change into a rule here.
- If no clip in Playbook fits a line, leave a marked gap the right length and tell the user, who can make one.

## Voiceover
- Cut every breath and every pause between phrases before timing anything (rulebook 4, first rule). The user's GIFT4HIM cut kept 36 pieces in 69s of speech, and the Founder ad's voice track is in 62 pieces. The voiceover is boosted (about +5 dB in CapCut), so it sits well on top.
- **Lines marked "Him/Her" in brackets are not voiced.** The bracketed line is played by a real B-roll clip of someone saying something like it, with the clip's own audio. Cut that line out of the voiceover and put the clip in its place. The voiceover comes back in on the next "Her" line.

## Pacing and B-roll
- A new shot on most caption phrases, usually 1.5–3.5s, changing on the same frame as the caption. Short one- or two-word captions share the next phrase's clip, and one continuous action can run across several captions (rulebook 6.20, 6.21). The reference ads have quick bursts on lists, but don't use them unless the user asks: the rulebook's 0.3s minimum shot applies (5.7).
- B-roll is literal to the phrase. For example, "rushing to the store" shows a store front, "grabbing whatever's left" shows socks, "spending 60 bucks" shows a red "-$60" over the shot, and "my 15-year-old rolls his eyes" shows a kid rolling his eyes.
- Mostly real people of all ages wearing and reacting to the light, many different creators, plus AI clips (`hf_…`). AI clips are zoomed 1.03 and often sped up 1.1–1.3x. Real clips are usually muted. Keep a clip's own sound only for a reaction line.
- Feature lines show the feature working: the 230° beam lighting up a whole room or garage, the wave sensor, the red light. Comparisons get small labels over the shot, such as "LUXELIGHT" in orange vs "OTHERS" in red.
- Marketplace or brand mentions get a logo popping over a blurred listing (Knockoff).
- Product and close: the box, a sack of boxes, pink and orange lights, and the correct black case with the white LUXE LIGHT logo. End on the light in hand.

## Text
- **Captions:** the house captions from rulebook 7 and 11a, in the user's CapCut caption look (11a, 2026-11-06), split and broken as in 7.8ab and 7.8ac. The reference ads' white and green caption boxes are not to be copied unless the user asks.
- **Checklists:** near the end, feature ticks stack up one per line ("✅ It's USB rechargeable").
- **End stack (last 2–3s), in place of the rulebook's "Click Shop Now + FREE CASE" box** (user, 2026-11-06, from Family Feud, one of the top spenders): three boxes appear together on "click shop now to get yours" and hold to the end. Captions stop as they appear.
  - "SHOP NOW FOR / UP TO 50% OFF 🎁": white box, black text, top (y 0.41 in CapCut).
  - "60-DAY GUARANTEE ✅": white box, black text (y -0.30).
  - "LOW STOCK ⚠️": red box, white text (y -0.46). Red draws the eye and signals urgency.
  - All three are Montserrat Bold, size 12 in CapCut, inside the 4:5 safe zone, with the click sound. The offer follows the brief (rulebook 8.5).
- **Hook text is the user's job, and they're still working out its look.** Don't copy the look of any hook title as a rule, and don't draft hook titles unless asked (user, 2026-11-06).
- **The hook voiceover gets captions too** (user, XMAS3HOOK, 2026-11-06), the same house captions as the body, split and timed the same way, on their own text track so they sit under the user's hook title boxes. The finished GIFT4HIM v3 had none, which the user treated as a mistake. `story_ad.py` still leaves the hook slot uncaptioned (it was built for rulebook 3.2), so caption the hook until the builder does: `christmas-3hook-vo/scripts/add_hook_captions.py` shows how.
- **Hook variants:** build them on the user's finished project with `tools/capcut/swap_hook.py`. It copies the project, swaps in the new hook's voice pieces (breaths left out), and moves the body along unchanged. It puts the placeholder card in the clip slot, for the user to replace, and leaves the hook text to the user. Done for GIFT4HIM Hooks B and C, 2026-11-06.
- **What the user changed on Hooks B and C (exported as Old husband V2 and V3, 2026-11-06):**
  - The hook clip goes on the main track, not an overlay. A hook can be two shots: in C, an AI clip runs under "men always say they don't want anything for Christmas", and the red light clip (06RedLight, 1.3x, zoomed 1.23) comes in on "they're lying, here's the proof".
  - A transition is allowed between the hook and the body: in B, CapCut's "Pull in" (0.47s) from the hook clip into the first body clip, with a "swish_whoosh (large)" starting 0.23s before the cut, at 0.32 volume.
  - The music can change per variant. B kept Christmas five but louder: 0.115 for the hook and the first 19s, then 0.074 from "socks and aftershave" on. C used "Commercial Break" at 0.02. Test music as a variable between hooks, not only the hook itself.
