# Video editing standards

**This is the main rulebook. It applies to every video in every style.** Each style has its own file in `Styles/<style>/style.md`, which adds to these rules and can override named defaults. The reviewer's latest notes beat both.

Paid social (Meta, TikTok) performance creative. Read fully before cutting anything. This is the one rulebook for video edits: `/ve-video-edit-general` follows it while cutting, `/apply-notes` follows it for anything a note leaves open (and writes new general rules back into it), and `/video-qa` checks finished renders against it.

Where a rule has a number, the number is the default, not a suggestion. Your reviewer's feedback beats this document; log what changed in section 12.

These rules started as the house standards of a performance creative agency, built from review notes on real ad edits. Make them yours: change any rule your team disagrees with, and let `/apply-notes` add new ones as you review.

**Tags.** Every rule says who handles it:
- `cut`: do it while editing.
- `script`: checked by code (a number or an exact match). *Built* means a script enforces it today; `video-qa` re-checks the finished MP4 after every render.
- `eye`: checked by looking at frames or listening.

---

## 1. What a performance edit is for

An ad has one job per second. The first second earns the second, the first three seconds earn the next ten, and everything after that earns the click. Edit backwards from that:

- The hook (0 to 3s) stops the thumb. It is judged on hold rate.
- The body (3s to about 25s) builds belief. It is judged on 50% and 75% completion.
- The close (last few seconds) names the action. It is judged on click-through.

If a cut does not serve one of those three, it is decoration. Remove it.

## 2. Default style: organic

1. **Default to organic** unless the brief says produced: no music unless the reviewer asks for it (section 9), no end card (the end box in section 10 is not one), no transitions, no stock footage, no sound effects except the end-box click and the story-ad sound effects in 6.49, and the house text from section 11a (never a display font like Montserrat Black). Captions are lowercase; hook boxes use sentence case or capitals, like the winning ads. `cut`
2. **Never mix organic and produced elements in one ad.** For example: organic text with polished branded before-and-afters, or a single lone greenscreen. `eye`

Sections 9 and 10 (music, end card) apply only when the brief asks for a produced ad.

3. **Style files may override named defaults, never the hook overlay.** For example, an "ugly organic" style skill might turn captions off (overrides 7.6), keep pauses up to about 1.5s (overrides the 0.25s cap in section 4), use no grade and no punch-ins (overrides 5.6) and let length follow the content. A skill that does this says so at the top of its SKILL.md and lists the sections it overrides. `cut`

## 3. Hook text overlay (highest priority, every video)

1. **Every video gets a text overlay on the hook that is not the captions.** Captions alone on the hook are never acceptable. The overlay says something the voice isn't saying: the premise, the category, the tension or the POV. This is how organic ads are run now. `cut` · `script` (built: `validate_plan.py` requires a `"hook": true` callout on the first line)
2. **While the hook overlay is on screen, hide the captions.** Captions start when it leaves. Show one text element at a time: no headline plus captions plus badge. `cut` · `script` (built: `render.py` drops captions under a hook callout)
3. **Hook overlay wording is plain, specific and readable at a glance.** Capitalise "I", "I'm" and proper names (Christmas) even when the hook is supplied in lowercase (reviewer, WHOSKIT v1). Break anything long onto two lines and make the key word biggest. **Never more than two lines of hook text in total**, across all its boxes (user, repeated 2026-11-08 after LUMI1108 v1 had four lines on Hook B and three on Hook C). If the idea doesn't fit in two lines, shorten it. Split a two-part headline into two bubbles (white-on-black, then black-on-white) so the second part pops. Never use internal abbreviations. `cut` · `eye`
4. **The hook overlay stays up until a natural break, then leaves on the exact frame of a camera cut,** at the first scene change (about 5 to 7 seconds in a skit, e.g. the cut to her opening the garage door), even if nobody speaks straight after it; the first caption then appears when the next word starts (reviewer, WHOSKIT v1). Applies to every hook. This also applies to hook text already burned into supplied footage: check it frame by frame, and if it ends early, hold it to the cut. A POV or premise headline can stay for the whole opener. `cut`
5. **The hook has to work muted** and be understood on first read. If the voice doesn't name the category in the first line, the overlay must. `eye`
6. **No logo, no brand name, no "hey guys" in the first three seconds.** `cut`
7. **A hook that is a claim beats a hook that is a question.** "This is why your skin still breaks out after a new routine" beats "Why does my skin keep breaking out?" `cut`

## 4. Pacing

- **Never an inhale or exhale in a voiceover (user, 2026-11-05, every style and brand).** Before any caption or clip is placed, cut every breath and all the dead air between phrases, including at the end of lines. Cut the whole pause, not down to a target gap: keep about 0.03s before the first sound and about 0.15s after the last. Breaths kill the flow and waste time. This comes first, because captions and B-roll are timed to the cut voiceover, so cutting breaths afterwards throws the whole timeline out of sync. It overrides the 0.15s / 0.10s gap targets below for voiceovers. `cut` · `script` (built: `tools/capcut/story_ad.py` `speech_pieces`, which keeps only sound that sits on a transcribed word, tuned on the user's own GIFT4HIM cuts. When the user re-cuts the voiceover in CapCut, read their pieces into `vo_pieces` and rebuild, never nudge the old timeline.) No voice slivers either: where a hook or range edge lands in the tail of a word, it leaves a 0.07 to 0.1s piece that plays as a click or half a breath, and the user deleted both in XMAS3HOOK (built: `story_ad.py` drops pieces under 0.15s). Listen to the end of every sentence too: an exhale hissing on after the last word ("plus the free case", XMAS3HOOK) is trimmed to just after the word. In CHEAPGENIUS (2026-11-07) the user still cut dead air by hand at four joins ("closet", "flashlight", "and said", "campsite, not one"), each about 0.1s of room tone after the word. Their kept tails run from 0 to 0.25s, so no fixed number reproduces this: listen to every join before delivery and trim any that sounds like a gap rather than a breath between sentences. Doing it in CapCut costs the user more than the time: a ripple delete shifts some text tracks and not others, which is how the end-stack boxes and the music ended up out of step.
- **Never cut a hook or voice piece before its last word has finished (user, 2026-11-07).** In CHEAPGENIUS V2 the hook "...because it only cost $17" ended at 11.77s, set from Whisper's word end (11.52) plus padding, but "seventeen dollars" runs on to 12.1s. The hook clipped "dollars" going into the body, and the user moved the cut to 12.23. Whisper's word ends run early, most of all on numbers and prices, which are spoken as several words. So take every piece's end from the forced-aligned word end (7.8aa), plus about 0.1s of tail, and never from Whisper's end. Then listen to the last word of every hook before delivery. Don't judge it by loudness alone: room tone and breaths sit at -45 to -50 dB, the same as a word fading out. `script` (built: `tools/capcut/swap_hook.py` `finish_words` moves a piece's end out to the aligned end of its last word, and leaves cuts that already fall in a pause alone. `story_ad.py` `compile_timeline` does the same to every hook and body range, and `speech_pieces` never drops sound inside an aligned word: in DELIVERY it cut 0.15s out of "seventy dollars", because the end of the word was quiet and Whisper ended it 0.4s early.)
- **Cut the voiceover to the sound, not to word timings: no audible gap, no clipped word (user, STOCKING 2026-11-10; this replaces the 0.18s/0.26s pause caps of 2026-11-09).** The pause caps still left gaps the user had to cut out, mostly at commas and full stops. That's faint sound after a word (its fading tail, a breath, room tone at -36 to -60 dB), which moves on the waveform but isn't heard. Then the onset fix cut into the starts of words, so they began late ("one", "usually", "laughed", "five"). Both came from cutting at aligned word timings plus fixed padding. The user's own cuts (STOCKING V1) follow the level, so cut the same way:
  - **End of a word:** cut about 0.03s after its sound drops below about -45 dB ("it." fades at 17.33, the user cut at 17.37; "Year's." 22.48 -> 22.50). A word ending on a stop ("dark.", "stock,", "down.", "up,") goes quiet for the closure and then releases, so carry on through a gap of up to 0.05s to keep the release. A click 0.08s after "card," isn't a release, and the user cut it.
  - **Start of a word:** start about 0.01 to 0.02s before its first sound ("and" begins 20.98, the user started at 20.97). A soft opening ("f", "th", "h") is quiet, so keep up to 0.08s of it down to -50 dB, but never a longer breath before the word.
  - **Every breath goes, and only the breath (user, STOCKING 2026-11-10: "how many times do I need to tell you").** Her breaths ran at -50 to -25 dB, louder than the -45 dB line, so the level-based trim kept them. A breath is its own burst of sound: the word fades, the level dips, then rises again for about 0.2s of unvoiced hiss that runs straight into the next word. In STOCKING V1 the user cut exactly three, after "laughed,", "once," and "light," (bursts of 0.17 to 0.28s at -36 to -45 dB, after a dip to -45 or -51). Cut from the dip to the next word's rising onset. Two things look like a breath and are not, and the user kept both: a word's own fading tail (the "s" of "face.", "case." and "since." runs on 0.15 to 0.3s and falls into silence), and a stop's release ("dark,", "dark.": a burst of about 0.12s after a short dip, then silence). The first version cut every unvoiced stretch at a comma or full stop and took 3.6s of word endings the user had kept, so never cut by voicing alone. The same goes before the first word of a hook or the body. `script` (built: `story_ad.py` `cut_breaths`, which matches the user's STOCKING V1 to the frame and prints every breath it cuts)
  - **A short word is not a sliver.** "The" in "with the free case" (FAKECHECK 2026-11-11) is a 0.06s burst with silence either side. The sliver rule dropped it and the word was lost. A short piece that holds a whole aligned word is kept. `script` (built: `story_ad.py` prints "kept a short word")
  - **The voice is only cut where it pauses for punctuation (user, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11).** The air to remove is at full stops and commas. Never cut between words inside a phrase: cutting between "60", "day" and "guarantee" made the close sound clippy. A cut whose last word has no comma or full stop is undone, unless there is a real pause there (0.25s or more). `script` (built: `story_ad.py` joins these and prints how many; `phrase_cuts: true` in the edit turns it off)
  - **Every word plays whole; no pause is added (user, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11, measured on their own cut of STYLE 3).** "Let it breathe" meant whole words, not longer gaps. They left 36 of 40 voice pieces exactly as cut and moved four by two or three frames: "free" and "50%" start 0.08 to 0.10s earlier, on the faint "fff" before the loud part of the word (visible in the waveform, about 10 dB above silence), and the one-word list items "pink", "orange", "free" end 0.06 to 0.07s later, where the fade reaches silence. So a soft opening is followed back to where the sound first leaves silence, and a short piece (under 0.6s) runs until its fade reaches silence. Full sentences keep the tighter end. A general pause at every cut (tried at 0.07/0.10s) made the ad 4s longer than their cut and is off. Voice pieces start on a whole frame, because CapCut moves a start up to its next frame on save and shaves the word. `script` (built: `sound_pieces` in `story_ad.py`; 37 of 40 pieces now land within a frame of the user's)
  - **Between words:** remove every silent dip of 0.04s or more, even mid-sentence ("way ... and", 0.05s at -52 to -66 dB).
  - **Check before delivery,** in the rendered audio: no stretch 30 dB under the speech level longer than about 0.07s, every piece starts before its word's sound and ends after it, and the transcript has every word.
  `script` (built: `story_ad.py` `sound_pieces`, the default trim. It matched every one of the user's STOCKING V1 edits within a frame, with no quiet stretch over 0.07s and no word lost)
- **The ad ends when the voiceover stops (user, 2026-11-07).** Video, B-roll, music and the end box all end on the last word, with no tail after it. `script` (built: `story_ad.py`, `tail` defaults to 0)
- **Target length.** Talking-head UGC: 25 to 45 seconds. B-roll plus voiceover: 20 to 35 seconds. Anything over 60 seconds needs a reason written in the brief. `script` (built: `video-qa`)
- **Gap between sentences: about 0.15s.** Gap between clauses inside a sentence: about 0.10s. Longer reads as hesitation; shorter sounds like a glitch. `cut` · `script` (built: `build_vo.py` tightens and reports)
- **No pause longer than 0.25s anywhere in the voice track**, except one deliberate beat before the close, which may run to 0.4s. `script` (built: `build_vo.py` prints `LOOSE PAUSES`)
- **A visual change every 2 to 3 seconds** in the body: a cut, a punch-in, a b-roll insert, a caption appearing, or a callout. `cut`
- **Don't hold a shot too long: about 4s maximum**, shorter on low-motion b-roll, or return to the talking head. `cut` · `script` (built: `validate_plan.py` warns above 4s)
- **Front-load.** The strongest proof or most surprising claim goes before the 8-second mark. Most of the audience never sees the end. `cut`
- **Trim room tone off every piece.** About 0.04s lead-in before the first word and about 0.06s tail after the last. Clipping a word's tail ("use", "pool", "free") is the most common audible mistake; when in doubt leave 0.15s of tail. `cut`

## 5. Cutting and structure

1. **Cut filler and throat-clearing:** leading "basically", "like", "okay so", "well", "hey everyone", "yeah", "if you don't know me", "that's it". Keep one only if the join sounds worse without it. "And", "so", "but" and "then" at a sentence start are judged in context: keep them when they carry the story (a contrast, a consequence, the next step), cut them when they are just a verbal run-up and the line reads the same without them. `video-qa` does not flag these four, so the cut is the editor's call. `cut` · `script` (built: `video-qa` flags filler at sentence starts)
2. **Cut repetition:** any claim, adjective or beat said twice, including the same word stacked across joins. `cut` · `script` (built: `video-qa` flags doubled words and repeated phrases)
3. **Start later than the creator did.** The first few seconds of a take are usually warm-up, so open on the first line that carries the premise. `cut`
4. **When building hooks or hook variants, scan the whole take** for the strongest 3–4 seconds (a reaction, a line, a moment), not only the scripted hooks. A great moment buried at the end is wasted. `cut`
4a. **Every version of an ad has the user's corrected body; only the hook changes (user, 2026-11-09).** When the user corrects the body in one version, every other version is rebuilt on that body before anything is exported or uploaded, including a version whose hook the user made by hand in an older project. CHEAPGENIUS V3 went out with the old B-roll the user had removed, because its hook was made in a project that still had the old body; the user has seen this a few times and called it unacceptable. Never pick the right body by "last edited": the stale project was the newest. Before delivering versions, and again before the user exports, run `check_bodies.py "<ad name>" --ref "<corrected version>"` and fix every version it lists as DIFFERENT BODY. `script` (built: `tools/capcut/check_bodies.py`; `swap_hook.py` refuses to build a variant while the versions' bodies differ)
4aa. **The versions workflow (user, STOCKING 2026-11-10).** 1) The user finishes V1 in CapCut: their fixes to my cuts, their own B-roll, the close. 2) I read what they changed (voice cuts, clips, boxes, sounds, filters) and save each correction as a rule. 3) I make V2 and V3 from that exact V1 body with `swap_hook.py`: only the hook changes, with its voice pieces, its captions and a draft hook box, and the hook slot on the main track is the blank card, so the user can lay their own hook B-roll on top. Never put my own clips in the hook slot: in STOCKING I picked hook clips and the user said they only want the slot to place their own. Each version gets its own song (5.4ab), and the project ends on the last clip. Run `check_bodies.py` on all three before handing over. `script`
4ab. **Every version gets a different song (user, STOCKING 2026-11-10).** V1, V2 and V3 never share a music track. Pick songs that fit the ad's theme: for a Christmas gift ad the user chose three Christmas tracks ("Christmas five", "Christmas Tree (Xmas Sales, Black Friday)", "X'mas Fun Jingle Bells"). The song is only a theme choice, so take the theme from the ad, not from this example. `cut`
4b. **The project ends on the last clip.** Nothing runs past the last video clip: CapCut exports to the end of the longest track, so music left running on plays over a black screen. STOCKING V1 (2026-11-10) had its music running 33s past the last clip. Check the project length against the last clip before every export. `script` (built: `swap_hook.py` cuts anything past the last clip in the versions it makes, and `check_bodies.py` lines versions up on the last clip)
5. **Cut on silence, not on timecode.** Find the gap in the waveform and cut there. Transcript word timings are a guide, not a cut point; they drift on restarts and repeated words. `cut`
6. **Jump cuts are the default** for talking head. Each one gets an alternating punch-in (about 1.0x then about 1.12x) so the frame changes with the audio. `cut`
7. **Minimum shot length is 0.3s.** Fold anything shorter into its neighbour; slivers of 1 to 7 frames flash like errors. `script` (built: `render.py` folds them and lists `CHECK FLASH CUTS`)
8. **Take selection:** energy and clarity first, then eye line, then background. Never splice two takes mid-word. `cut`
9. **Remove stumbles, restarts and fillers, then listen to the splice on its own.** A repair that produces "now we, now we" or a half "and" is worse than the original stumble. Never clip the first or last word of a line. `cut` · `script` (built: `build_vo.py` re-transcribes and diffs against the script)
10. **Default: don't cut away during the number.** If the creator says "47%" or "three weeks", the face or the proof stays on screen. Exception: a reviewer can ask for b-roll of people using the product over an offer line. `cut`

## 6. Footage and b-roll

1. **B-roll answers "show me".** Insert it when the voice makes a claim that can be seen: texture, before and after, mechanism, unboxing, size in hand. The footage must literally show what the line says. `cut` · `script` (built: `validate_plan.py` requires `illustrates` and `shows`)
2. **Use the best clip available, not the first one that fits.** Reject dark, blurry, crooked, over-exposed or dull clips. `eye`
3. **Flip mirrored footage.** Front-camera clips show text backwards on phones, boxes and bags, so check any clip containing text. `eye`
4. **Check the background and edges of every shot** for clutter and distractions (socks, studio lights, a TV, a shower caddy over the brand name). Crop, reframe or swap. `eye`
5. **The person on screen matches the persona in the script** (age, gender, situation). Keep the same creator on "I" lines. `eye`
6. **Order of preference:** product in use by the creator > product in use by anyone > product close-up > lifestyle context > screen recording. No stock in organic ads (section 2). `cut`
7. **Ratio and holds.** Talking-head body: 30 to 50% b-roll coverage. B-roll holds 1.5 to 3 seconds. `cut`
8. **Cut b-roll on motion.** Enter a clip while something is moving (a pour, a hand reaching). Entering on a still frame looks like a slideshow. `cut`
9. **Picture-in-picture** is for keeping the creator visible while a demo plays: rounded corners, bottom-left or bottom-right, never covering a caption. `cut`
10. **Verify in-points at quarter-second resolution.** Contact sheets at two-second steps misplace shot changes by about a second. `cut`
11. **B-roll enters between lines, not mid-sentence.** Start an insert on the line's first word (or in the gap before it); switching halfway through a sentence feels like a harsh cut. Exceptions are fine when they're deliberate, e.g. a list where each item gets its own shot, or the product shown as it's named. When a line is meant to be covered, cover it from its start so the A-roll isn't seen for it. Don't leave a flash of A-roll (under about 0.5s) between two inserts; it reads as a glitch. `cut`
11a. **Prefer real people over AI footage when both exist, and show the whole product when showing a feature.** Don't cut to a stranger on a personal line when the speakers can carry it. `cut`
11b. **Colour-correct HDR iPhone clips (HLG) before use,** or they look flat and tinted. `eye`
12. **Show a wearable feature on a person using it,** not on a surface (e.g. a wave sensor being used on someone's head). `cut`
13. **Don't reuse a clip, or a near-identical one, in the same ad.** Two takes of the same prop in the same style (e.g. two handheld shots of the same old product) count as the same clip. If nothing else fits, stay on the creator. `cut` · `eye`
14. **The user's go-to clips are preferred, rotated so no two ads look the same (user, 2026-11-10; this replaces "always pick different clips").** For each kind of line, start from the clips the user has picked for it before (the brand's clip-picks file and clip log, rule 54). Where they have used two or more clips for that beat, take one the last ad didn't use. Where there is only one, reuse it. Go outside their picks only when the line needs something they don't cover, or the clip is already in this ad (rule 13). Only library clips count as picks: the voiceover, the user's hooks and AI clips made for one ad are never go-tos. Their patterns shift over time, so after every V1 they finish, log it with `tools/capcut/learn_picks.py add` and update the clip-picks file from `learn_picks.py report`. `cut` · `script`
14a. **No shot appears twice in one ad (user, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11).** The same moment of a clip never plays twice, zoomed in or not. A different part of a long clip that shows something different is fine: in their own cut the user used `Fakes/IMG_8655` twice (the fake's box cropped in, then both lights wide) and `Packaging/IMG_2731` twice (48s and 5s in). When the footage for a line runs out, put a stand-in on that line and tell the user which line it is and why, so they can judge it or supply a clip. Never fill the gap by replaying something already shown. `cut` · `script` (check the shot list for repeated files and overlapping in-points before building)
14c. **A line that names the product and then a feature gets two shots (user's cut, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11).** "The real LuxeLight 2.0 / has a red light mode": a clean shot of the product with its logo on the name, then a cut to the feature already happening on its own words, with a sound on the cut. One clip running under both, starting on something else (white light), was replaced. `cut`
14d. **The clip shows what the line says, readably (user's cut, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11).** "Hunting" got a hunter, "see clearly in the dark" got a shot where you can see clearly, "the way white light does" got a horse reacting to white light. A dark or abstract clip of the right feature (a red glare, a near-black silhouette) was swapped every time. No creator talking to camera as B-roll in a voiceover ad. `cut` · `eye`
14b. **A reference ad's pace is never a reason to add cuts (user, FAKECHECK 2026-11-11).** Cuts stay where this rulebook puts them: on a new idea, a new item in a list or a step of an action. Don't split a phrase to get a faster cut ("the 230-degree" / "wide beam" was rejected as neither necessary nor productive). From a reference, take the look: title cards, black and white, push-ins, graphics, the close. `cut`
15. **Show the product being used on claims about how it performs,** so viewers can picture themselves using it. A product shot on a surface suits a named feature like "230 degree beam". Show the beam in the dark or strongly lit, not in daylight, where it has no impact. `cut`
15a. **Check it's actually our product before using a clip.** Other headlamps appear in Playbook, some in the same locations as ours. Look for the wide strip across the forehead on a close frame. A round spot of light means it's probably another torch. `eye`
16. **Match the proof to the line.** "So light": someone holding it up on their hand. Scales only when the line names the weight. "Clunky" or "heavy": the old bulky torches being handled roughly so they look heavy. `cut`
17. **When a shot sells the product's features, show the product clearly on the person, switched on and facing camera.** Someone with their back turned doing the activity doesn't show the feature. `cut` · `eye`
18. **Use a mix of people across an ad's shots when the footage allows.** Don't fill every shot with the same creator when there are other relevant clips. `cut`
19. **The hook clip shows the persona's activity at a glance** (a camping ad opens at a campsite), not a generic shot of the product. `cut`

**How the user picks and times B-roll (GIFT4HIM walkthrough, user, 2026-11-06, every style):**

20. **Shots hold about 3.5s at most.** Go up to 4s, never more, only when the clip is full of movement (colour swatches being thrown about). A still or slow clip that long is dull. On a long line held by one clip, add a slow punch-in partway through, or cut the same clip into two different angles so it feels like a new scene. `cut`
21. **No clip so short it can't register.** A one- or two-word caption ("plus", "this year") doesn't get its own clip. The clip runs on across it into the next caption. One clip can also run across several captions when it shows one continuous action, like a wave on and off. Not every caption change needs a cut, but every cut is on a caption change (7.8b). `cut`
22. **Dead clips are wasted.** If nothing happens in a clip ("and check this" over a still hand), start the next demo clip earlier instead, for example showing the button press from "and check this". `cut`
23. **Show exactly the line, and check it really happens in the clip.** "Press the button once" needs the button actually pressed on screen. "Greasy hands or gloves" needs someone in gloves, or wiping their hands, using the wave sensor. "Lit up one tiny spot" needs a cheap headlamp's small spot beam, which should look bad. "Turning his head to see" shows the head turning. "Flies drawn to white light" shows bugs at a white light. A feature beat lands on its word: the switch to red light happens on "switch". `cut` · `eye`
24. **Wave sensor clips show the wave on and the wave off in the same shot,** before the scene changes. Speed the clip up (1.4 to 1.7x) to fit both into the line, rather than cutting off the return wave. Skip clips where the light goes on as the voice says "off". If nothing lines up, a clip of someone waving it on and off repeatedly is fine. `cut`
25. **Speed a slow clip up so the whole action fits the line,** rather than cutting it off. Slow one slightly (about 0.9x) when the clip's own next scene would otherwise creep in at the end. `cut`
26. **Keep the story's characters consistent and believable.** Know who the script is about (for example, a woman married 20 years buying for her husband) and show that demographic: an older man for "he" lines, an older couple for "20 years together". The bare minimum is the right person doing the thing: no woman in a stable for "his night vision". Come back to the same man across the ad where you can, and don't jump between strangers on lines about one person. Avoid people who contradict it (a young man for an older husband, or a gift tag saying "dad" on a husband line, which should be cropped out). When no clip fits, use the closest and tell the user, who makes the missing clip. `cut` · `eye`
27. **No two clips of the same thing back to back with different people** (two different men opening socks). They read as one repeated beat with a cast change. `cut`
28. **Only our captions on screen.** Clips found online (TikTok, snaptik) often have their own captions, CapCut template text or watermarks. Use a part of the clip without them, or crop and zoom them out of frame. If neither works, don't use the clip. `eye`
29. **AI clips (Higgsfield, `hf_…`) go at 1.03 scale,** because they sometimes have thin black bars at the edges. Speed slow ones up about 1.1x, and cut before a moment that looks fake. `cut`

## 7. Captions

0. **One caption engine for every video with speech and captions,** whatever the style or brand: voiceover, skit, BOF, talking head or anything new. Build captions, hook and end boxes, and caption timing with `~/Video Editing Test/tools/captions/house_captions.py`, and run its `check_spans` before rendering. It fails on blanks, on cuts that aren't on a caption change, and on captions over two lines. Never write new caption code, and never copy a reference ad's caption look over it. Styles with no captions, like Benefit Stack chips, are exempt. A style may only change what its style file names (user, 2026-11-05). `cut` · `script` (built: `check_spans`)
1. **Captions match the spoken words exactly.** After rendering, check the caption words against a transcription of the final audio, separately from the voice-track check. Catch ghost words that survive a cut and doubled words ("every every"). `script` (built: `video-qa` compares captions with a transcript of the final audio)
2. **Brand, product and URL names always appear in their exact house form, every time.** Run a find-and-replace from the brand's name list in `brand-house-style.md` before export (`fix_words` in the plan). `script` (built: `fix_words` while cutting; `video-qa` checks every caption and overlay against the list)
3. **Spelling follows the brand's market (US or UK), not the creator's accent.** The market is in `brand-house-style.md`. `script` (built: `video-qa`)
4. **House style for numbers and units:** digits with units (45 lbs, 100+, 12 MINUTES, 500 LEDs) and $ on dollar amounts; hyphenated compound modifiers (2-in-1, 90-day money-back guarantee, follow-up, walk-in); "drugstore" as one word. `script` (built: `video-qa`)
5. **Captions are lowercase with no punctuation at line ends, except a question mark on a question.** Keep proper names as written (LuxeLight, USB, Christmas) and capitalise "I". `cut`
6. **Captions on every spoken word after the hook overlay leaves,** in sync with the voice, in short phrases of up to two lines, at one fixed position (section 11a). No active-word highlight. No emoji in captions, except a deliberate one for a joke. `cut`
7. **Keep text on screen with no blanks.** Each caption holds until the next one starts, even through a pause, and the first caption appears the moment the hook overlay leaves. A caption only changes once the current speaker has finished. `cut`
8. **List items get their own caption, one at a time, with no commas** ("camping", then "fishing"). `cut`
8a. **Split captions at commas and spoken pauses so viewers can't read ahead of the voice** ("but I'm thinking", then "if we want to"). No commas on screen. Find the pause in the audio, not the grammar: the voice may pause after a small word, so the split follows it ("now you could try to" then "hunt down a different headtorch", user, 2026-11-06). `cut`
8ab. **Split where the sentence has its natural break, and leave them wanting the rest** (user, 2026-11-06). "Not only can he" then "switch to red light mode". "This year I didn't want to" then "break the bank": the viewer asks "didn't want to what?", which holds attention. Never the whole sentence in one caption, because they read ahead and drop off. But don't chop so fine that a caption flashes past ("this year" alone, 0.5s). Join it to the next words. `cut`
8ac. **Line breaks (user, 2026-11-06).** Judge by the caption's width on screen, not its character count: digits and capitals are wide. "The 230-degree wide beam" (24 characters) ran into the Instagram buttons on the right, while "they're actually running" (also 24) fits. In CapCut's system font at size 100, one line is up to about 1200 units wide (`text_w` in `story_ad.py`): every caption the user kept on one line measured 1184 or less, and every one they broke measured 1234 or more. Up to that width fits on one line ("switch to red light mode"). Anything longer is broken by hand into two lines, so it never runs wide into the platform's buttons at the sides. Make the two lines as even as possible ("because his / old headlamp", "so they stay / out of his face"). Never top heavy, with a long top line over one or two words. If they can't be even, a longer bottom line is better. Keep words that belong together on one line, and split the caption instead when they can't be ("this has a", then "230-degree wide beam"). Two stacked lines are fine when the voice reads at the speed of the text. Split into two captions when the line is long and the voice is slower than reading. `cut` · `script` (built: `balance_lines` in `tools/capcut/story_ad.py`)
8aa. **A caption changes on the exact frame its first word starts, through the whole ad.** "Exactly when. Not after or before a little bit. Bang on" (user, 2026-11-06). Take the start from the audio itself, not the transcript's timings. Whisper's word starts run early in running speech: it puts a word's start on the tail of the word before, so a caption timed from it comes up before the word is heard and the viewer reads ahead. The right frame is the word's attack, the frame before the voice jumps into the word, or, when the words run together, the quietest point between them. Never back up from the transcript start into the word before. The B-roll for that line starts on the same frame, so a late or early caption is also a late or early cut. In XMAS3HOOK v1, 11 of 32 captions came in 0.1 to 0.3s early and the user re-timed every one by hand. CHEAPGENIUS v1 still had six lines 3 to 9 frames off ("his whole family", "and nothing impresses him", "and started", "with greasy hands", "are his sister's thing", "as her phone"), some early and some late. In each case Whisper's boundary was wrong and the attack search only looked near it. Since 2026-11-07, word starts come from a forced alignment of the transcript to the audio (wav2vec2), and the caption goes 0.03s before the aligned start. On the user's 51 CHEAPGENIUS marks, 47 land within a frame and 50 within two, against 13 of 24 mid-line captions within a frame for the old method. Lines that open a voice piece still snap to the piece (7.8ad). `script` (built: `tools/capcut/align_words.py`, used by `story_ad.py` `aligned_words`, cached as `<transcript>.aligned.json`. The skit template's `voice_onset` still uses the old method.)
8ad. **When a line starts a new voiceover piece, the caption, the B-roll and the voice piece start on the same tick** (user, 2026-11-06, "complete congruency"). On the timeline the three edges form one straight line. Not a frame before, not a frame after, and not at the word inside the piece. Line them up to where the new voice piece starts. It is neater, and it can't drift out of sync. `script` (built: `story_ad.py` snaps caption and shot starts to the piece start and fails the build if any is off)
8b. **B-roll starts and ends on a caption change, on the same frame.** A caption never spills from one shot into the next. `cut`
8c. **Text changes land on the footage's own cuts:** the hook leaves and the end box arrives on a camera cut, and b-roll never ends a frame or two before one. `cut`
9. **Don't switch caption colour mid-video** without a reason (e.g. a second speaker, applied throughout). `eye`

## 11. Variations, delivery and QC

1. **Every concept ships with four hooks:** A is the scripted one; B, C and D are variations with different opening visuals (Meta groups visually similar ads under one Entity ID). **The reviewer decides how many hooks to build and when.** If they ask for several at once, build them all on the same body. Otherwise build A first and the rest once A is approved. Hooks differ only in the opening, unless the reviewer asks for something else such as music on some versions. `cut`
1a. **When applying notes, change only what a note names.** Leave every other clip, line and text as it was, even ones a nearby note's general comment could apply to. If a note is unclear about which shot it means, ask on the note. `cut`
2. **A fix on one variation is applied to every variation.** The hand-off includes a per-variation checklist confirming each requested change on A, B, C and so on. `cut`
3. **Flag any competitor branding visible in the footage** (logos, labels, shop signs, clothing). Don't use the clip, or blur the branding. `eye`
4. **Formats:** 9:16 at 1080x1920, 30fps, H.264 about 12 Mbps, AAC 192kbps; 4:5 at 1080x1350 when asked. Name files `<CODE>_Hook<X>_v<N>.mp4`, e.g. `ACM08_HookB_v3.mp4` (or your own ad naming convention).
5. **Every delivery includes** the hook cuts, the gap report on the voice track, the safe-zone result, a frame-by-frame look at any shot under 0.4s, the headline-duration choice (3.4) and the per-variation checklist (11.2).

**Review checklist before anything ships**

1. Watch it muted. Does the hook overlay stop you, and does the story still make sense?
2. Listen with eyes closed. Any stumble, filler, repetition, clipped word or gap over 0.25s?
3. Captions: exact words, house brand names, market spelling, number style, sentence casing, hidden under the hook overlay.
4. Scrub for slivers and zoom flicker. Any shot under 0.3s, or over 4s?
5. Every clip: relevant, best available, not mirrored, clean background, persona matches, no competitor branding.
6. Text bounds inside the safe zone (the 4:5 box); one text element at a time.
7. Organic stayed organic: no music, end card, transitions, SFX or stock.
8. Every requested fix is on every variation.

## 11a. House look, timing, b-roll and music (every style)

Learned over six rounds of reviewer notes on REDSKIT. The working code for all of it is `tools/captions/house_captions.py` (7.0), which the skit template and the voiceover builder both import.

**Start from the reference.** If the reference ad has a CapCut project (`~/Movies/CapCut/User Data/Projects/com.lveditor.draft`, search `draft_info.json` for the hook wording), take the font, colours, sound effects and music volume from it. Don't estimate them from frames.

**Hook and end boxes**
- Font is CapCut's system font (Metropolis, `/Applications/CapCut.app/Contents/Resources/Font/SystemFont/en.ttf`) with CapCut's bold, not Proxima Nova or Montserrat. Size 75 at 1080 wide, plus a thin bold stroke (about 0.67px).
