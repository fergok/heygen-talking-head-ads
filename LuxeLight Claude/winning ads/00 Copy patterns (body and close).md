# How the winning ads say it: bodies and closes

From the 15 files in this folder, all story-led top- and mid-funnel video ads, with no BOF offer ads (2026-11-10). Read this before writing any body or close. The hooks are covered elsewhere. This file is about what the body says and how it says it.

Use these as **ways of saying things**, not lines to paste. Never reuse a line from one of our own earlier scripts (for example the "sci-fi movie" line). Write fresh lines in these patterns.

---

## 1. The body moves through five beats

| Beat | What it does | How the winners say it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The dull default** | Names the boring gifts or the old way, concretely | "the same boring socks, gift cards and presents that end up forgotten in a drawer" (Grown Men) · "Everyone else brought candles and gift cards" (Family Feud) · "spending 60 bucks for half empty stockings" (Stocking Stuffer) · "socks and aftershave he'll never use" (Old husband) · "Expected a polite 'oh thanks sweetie' and the junk drawer" (dadgift) · toiletries, socks, aftershave ranked (Ranking) |
| **2. The turn** | One short line where she does something different | "But not this year." · "I brought five of these LuxeLight headlamps." · "So I took a chance and bought 5." · "This year I bought FIVE." · "So I got him a Luxe Light." |
| **3. One person, one feature, one place** | Each relative gets one feature, shown through their job or habit | Dad on the car with greasy hands → wave sensor · husband in the garage → 230° · brother on runs and camping → red light · mom on the dog walk → pink and the leash · nephew in construction → better than the company headlamp · grandma knitting without waking grandpa |
| **4. Spread and reaction** | Proof told as what people did, never as a claim | "couldn't believe how much he could see" · "the whole room went quiet" · "laughing like little kids" · "comes back 10 minutes later still wearing it" · "everyone sending me videos" · "Mom, where did you find this?" · "everyone kept borrowing mine" · "the neighbor he showed it to wants one" |
| **5. The payoff line** | One summing-up line with a number or a callback | "4 Gifts. 4 happy people. Not one left in a drawer." · "5 completely different people. And somehow the same gift worked for all of them." · "Christmas shopping done and nobody's getting my pink one." · "Best gift I've given him in 20 years." · "Best accidental gift I've ever given." |

## 2. How each feature is said

**Wave sensor.** The most-used wording, in this rhythm:
- "Just wave your hand. Light on. Wave again. Off. No fumbling with buttons." (Grown Men, Family Feud)
- Always tied to a reason the hands are busy: greasy hands, gloves, carrying tools, a leash, "covered in grease", "my hands are filthy".
- The accurate version: "press the button once to switch on the wave sensor. Then just wave your hand…" (Old husband)

**230° beam.** Always set against one spot, then a place:
- "lights up your whole work area instead of one tiny spot like regular headlamps"
- "lights up the whole path, not just like a phone torch right in front of you"
- The knock-on benefit: "without constantly turning your head" / "forever turning his head to see" / "could see the bolt and the wrench beside it without chasing either with my head"
- The places are the garage, the whole path, the workbench, the hallway and the whole area in front of me.

**Red light.** Said as a benefit to a person, never as a spec:
- "He even discovered the red light mode, which helps preserve night vision when you're out camping." (Grown Men)
- "switch to red light mode, which helps protect his night vision… flies don't get drawn to red light the way they do with white, so they stay out of his face." (Old husband)
- "The white light attracts bugs in my face and I'm chewing them" (Red Light Skit)
- "softer on their eyes, it's not going to startle them" (horses, Red Light Skit)
- "the red light keeps the bugs out of my face" (Gift Song)
- Light-touch versions: "There's a red mode too." / "Oh, for camping." (5 Gifts) · "It's even got a red light mode." (Snow)
- Brand rule: say it switches from white light to red light.

**Colours.** Handed out per person, scarce, and tied to identity:
- "Black for dad and my husband, orange for my brother." … "So I got her the pink one for walking the dog."
- "limited edition colors… pink and orange. Which honestly make great gifts if you want to pick the color to match the person."
- "They've just done limited edition pink and orange, so I've matched one to each of them."
- Possessiveness: "nobody's getting my pink one" · "No, you're not having this one" · "I'm not letting him near the pink one"
- Reveal as a feature: "An orange LuxeLight?" / "Yes, an orange LuxeLight, limited edition."

**The small extras**, one short line each, never stacked:
- Case: "every LuxeLight comes with a free carry case so they're basically gift ready" · "gift ready out of the box"
- Size: "Small enough to fit in a stocking"
- Charging: "charges with USB-C" · "charges off the same cable as his phone" · "Held up the USB cable. Those days are over."
- Weight: "I forget it's even on my head" · "lighter than his reading glasses"
- Fit: "fits right over my cap" · "fit over any hard hats or beanies"
- Water: "all 3 grown men were outside splashing water on their headlamps"

## 3. The voice

- **Short, spoken sentences.** Fragments are fine: "Day 2: garage at 11pm. Day 4: walking the dog in the dark."
- **Specific people with jobs or habits**, never "my family": "my nephew who works construction", "grandma knits at night without waking grandpa".
- **Present-tense scenes** in the middle: "My husband tries the motion sensor, waves his hand…"
- **Real numbers:** "5 Christmas gifts, all for under $90", "10 minutes later", "the $50 headlamp his company bought him", "20 years together".
- **Light humour and slang:** "godsend", "Mayor of sexy town", "bloody walk the dogs", "Jackie Chan wave".
- **Dialogue snippets** carry the proof: "Where did you get that?", "Babe! It lights up everything.", "Mom, where did you find this?"

## 4. How the closes work

Most winning closes have three or four parts, in this order:

1. **The offer, given a reason inside the story** (the strongest version):
   - "Right now they've got up to 50% OFF, which is how I managed to grab five of them." (Family Feud)
   - "they're up to 50% off right now, the only reason I could afford four." (dadgift)
   - "They're also doing 50% off right now because I looked it up to buy one for myself." (Ranking)
   - Plain version: "Today only, get up to 50% off." / "Up to 50% off plus free carry cases."
2. **Colour scarcity:** "Pink and orange are limited editions, so tap shop now before they're gone." · "The colors are still in stock." · "before the limited edition colors are gone"
3. **A persona callout** (optional): "So if your husband, dad, or brother keeps saying he wants nothing for Christmas, get him one of these." · "If your dad's still heading out every winter with some old dim headlamp, get him something he'll actually use." · "So if you're still shopping for your family this Christmas…"
4. **An emotional callback to the hook:** "Best gift I've given him in 20 years. And honestly, he'll tell everyone I nailed it." · "I told her not to waste her money. Now all the family's getting one." · "Best Christmas gift I've given that everyone actually bought." · "One gift, and he'll remember who always knew him best."

Extras that show up: the free carry case (nearly always), the 60-day money-back guarantee ("If they don't use it, get your money back"), and "selling out fast this Christmas".

## 5. Must-haves for every new script (user, 2026-11-10)

- **Work in the colours:** black, orange and pink, given per person, with pink and orange called limited editions.
- **Work in the red light**, as a benefit to a person (night vision, no bugs in the face, not startling animals, not blinding people). Say it switches from white light to red light.
- Write fresh lines in these patterns. Never copy lines from our own earlier scripts.
