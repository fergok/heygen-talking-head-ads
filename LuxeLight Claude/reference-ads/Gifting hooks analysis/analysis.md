Analysed 2026-11-07 with the `creative-winning-variables` skill. Source: Parker, lifetime, Meta default attribution, GBP.

## Evidence

**What I searched:** every ad-name group with £20k+ lifetime spend matching "gift": 51 groups, all read.
- I dropped the ones that aren't gifting ads: BOF Colour Apology, Dark Nights, Prime Day, Ferg's Disaster, They Copied Us, Runner, Case Offer, Sorry Tradesmen, Aus Launch.

**What I worked from:**
- Parker scripts for all 16 gifting video concepts.
- Frames from the first 4 seconds of the 9 biggest (`evidence/hook_*.jpg`).

## Finding 1: you're right, the hook is the variable

Three pairs share a word-for-word body and only the hook changes:

| Pair | Hook A | Hook B | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grown Men Gifts | "I spent $80 on Christmas gifts for three grown men. I've never seen them this excited." | "Three men in my family told me not to buy them anything… now they won't take their presents off." | A: £372.6k at 1.94. B: £256.8k at 1.82 |
| Family Feud | "$30 gift → chaos at family Christmas" | "Dirty Santa… big mistake" / "favourite in-law" | V2 £102k. V1 and V3 about £60 each |
| Panicked | "My wife panicked and grabbed this…" (USA 2025) | "My kids panicked… for Father's Day" (AUS 2026) | 1.68 vs 1.70. Same structure, different market |

The bodies are near-copies of each other across Grown Men, Family Feud and Stocking Stuffer:
- "Just wave your hand. Light on. Wave again. Off. No fumbling with buttons."
- The 230° "whole work area, not one tiny spot".
- Mom and the dog walk.
- "Free carry case… gift ready".
- The socks, gift cards and "drawer" alternatives.

**The body is a shared, proven asset. The hook decides how far an ad scales.**

## Finding 2: hook rate doesn't tell you who buys

- The highest hook rates were ProudWife (71–80%) and Grown Men 4 (72%). Both sit below the top ROAS.
- Grown Men 1 beat Grown Men 4 on spend and ROAS with a **20-point lower** hook rate.
- Gift Sale has the **lowest** hook rate (30.6%) and the **highest** ROAS (2.09).

The emotional, story-heavy ads get the most attention but don't convert as well. SongProudWife, Gift Song, Sewing and ProudWife have the biggest holds (38–43%) and CTRs (6–8%), but their ROAS is 1.56–1.72 and their AOV is under £50. They pull in watchers and clickers, not people buying five.

**Implication:** when you go after emotion and comments, the hook still has to point at *buying several*. Otherwise you get a viral watch and a weak CPA.

## Finding 3: what the hooks that make people buy have in common

### A. The narrator is the person giving, not the person receiving

Compare the two groups, each with about £860k of spend:
- **A woman giving to several people** (Grown Men 1 and 4, Family Feud, Gifts for Women, Stocking Stuffer): **£859.8k at 1.87 ROAS.**
- **The receiver talking about one gift** (ProudWife, both Panicked ads, SongProudWife, ListIdea, Ranking, UGC Skit, SKIT JACK): **£863.8k at 1.66 ROAS.**

**Why it matters:** the buyer is a 55+ woman. When she hears a woman like her being the hero of Christmas, she sees herself. When she hears a man saying "my wife got me this", she's being told about a gift, not shown herself giving one.

### B. More than one recipient, with a number, in the hook

- The hooks that convert best put a group and a figure in the first line: "three grown men", "$80", "$30", "stocking gifts", "I bought FIVE", "more than one guy", "5 gifts for under $90".
- AOV follows the hook. Hooks with several recipients get £53–61. Single-recipient hooks get £37–50.
- At similar CPAs (£28–32), that AOV gap is most of the ROAS gap.

### C. Frame one shows the payoff, not the problem

- **Grown Men 1:** an older man in orange hi-vis reads the paper calmly with his lamp lit, while two younger men wrestle over theirs, lit, in front of the Christmas tree. Three people, three lamps, a reaction, from frame 1. **Family Feud V2 reuses this composition.**
- **Gifts for Women:** her sister is laughing at the camera while she hangs ornaments in a pink lamp.
- **Grown Men 4** opens on three sceptical talking-head selfies (the problem) and only cuts to the wrestling payoff at 3.5s. It got a higher hook rate but fewer buyers.
- **ProudWife** opens on a slow kiss on the head. **Xmas gift v1** opens on water poured over the lamp. Neither shows a person receiving a gift.

### D. The emotion is surprise and delight, with a small twist

- The winning hooks set something low ($30, $80, "don't buy me anything", "a headlamp… great") against an over-the-top reaction ("chaos", "act like kids", "won't take them off").
- **Hypothesis:** what does the work is the gap between how small the gift seems and how big the reaction is, not any single emotion.

## Reusable pattern for a gifting hook (instructions)

1. A woman giving is the narrator. She's the hero, not the person receiving.
2. Name more than one recipient and a number in the first line (a price, a count, or both).
3. Set a low expectation against an outsized reaction.
4. Frame one shows several people already wearing lit lamps and reacting, in a Christmas home. No product-only shots.
5. Then drop straight into the proven body: wave-sensor demo → 230° whole work area → mom's dog walk → gift-ready free case → "X gifts, X happy people".

## New hook concepts

Each one keeps the proven body and the A–D rules above. **The only thing that changes is the emotion the hook lands.** That makes them one-variable tests that can run against Grown Men 1 (1.94 ROAS, £28.47 CPA) as the control.

```
Stacked winners carried in: female giver narrator; several recipients plus a number in the hook; payoff in frame one (several people in lit lamps in a Christmas home); the Grown Men / Feud body word for word
Variable under test: the emotion of the hook (below)
Win condition: ROAS ≥ 1.94 and CPA ≤ £28.47 at £5k+ spend (suggested)
Kill condition: ROAS < 1.75 at £5k, or Meta won't spend (< £500 in 4 days) (suggested)
Metrics: ROAS, CPA, AOV (bar: £53+), hook rate (bar: 50%+), comment count
Type: Deliberate
```

## Gaps

- **A clean giver-vs-receiver test.** The same body narrated both ways, in one ad set. This would turn finding 3A from a strong pattern into a proven Winner.
- **Break-even ROAS.** Needed before any ad can be tiered.
- **Per-market splits.** Some ads ran only in AUS or the USA, so the market sits alongside the hook.

## Hook-rate caution
The pure offer and BOF ads have **low hook rates but the highest ROAS**, because they're mostly retargeting warm audiences. Examples: Pink Flash Sale 2.94 at a 25% hook rate, the Apology "5,000 units left" version 2.92 at 46%, and Do NOT Buy 2.62 at 26%. Don't copy their hook rates as a target for a cold gifting hook.

## Text hook mechanics, ranked by evidence

### 1. Confess a mistake, or say someone did something wrong
**Proof:**
- SORRY TO EVERYONE WHO BOUGHT LAST WEEK: £310k at 2.13.
- THIS MISTAKE COST US $$$ 😡: 2.40 ROAS, 72% hook.
- FIRING OUR TOP EMPLOYEE 😡: 2.01.
- WE MADE A HUGE MISTAKE: 2.39.
- I bought 5 of the same gift and thought I'd RUINED Christmas 😬: **2.08, AOV £62**.
- I should've bought him this sooner 😔: 2.01, 73% hook.

**Why it works:** the viewer has to find out whether it really was a mistake. This is the most consistent winner in the account.

### 2. A number and a price set against a big reaction
**Proof:**
- $80… GROWN MEN ACT LIKE KIDS 😳: £361k at 1.93.
- THE $30 GIFT THAT NEARLY ENDED XMAS 😳: 1.81.
- THE $30 GIFT THAT TOOK OVER XMAS 🎄: 1.83, 76% hook.
- 5 CHRISTMAS GIFTS FOR UNDER $100?! 😳: **2.09, AOV £62.81**.
- I went to buy one gift and left with FIVE 🛒: **2.57, AOV £61**.

**Why it works:** "five" or "under $100" in the hook pushes AOV up.

### 3. A relationship-drama label, plus a twist in brackets
**Proof:**
- Jealous husband unlocked 👀 (over this of all things): 1.84–1.92 ROAS, 74% hook, 43–44% hold.
- Divorce papers are being drafted 🤣: 1.90.
- Ranking my wife's gifts 🎁 (this didn't go well): 1.72–1.90.
- ARGUMENT INCOMING…: up to 2.15 ROAS, 76% hook.

**Why it works:** a couple in conflict, and the bracket hints at the payoff.

### 4. Forbidden or reverse
**Proof:**
- DO NOT buy a LuxeLight…: 2.62.
- STOP BUYING LUXELIGHT (we're almost sold out): 2.02.

### Emoji
Winners nearly always end on one emotional emoji: 😳 😬 👀 😡 🤣 😔.

## Visual hook patterns

**What wins: an event or a strange juxtaposition in frame one.** Something is *happening*:
- Men wrestling over lamps (Grown Men 1).
- A man cutting the headlamp with pink scissors (Apology, the top spender).
- A knife over the pink headlamp (STOP BUYING).
- An angle grinder throwing sparks (Ferg's Disaster 3).
- A boardroom crisis (Ferg's Disaster 2).
- A man falling face first into snow (Snow).
- A woman holding a tower of five wrapped gifts (5 Gifts).
- Five stockings lined up (stocking filler).
- Dad carving the turkey in a lit lamp (dadgift).
- A husband walking in with the box while his wife is mid-chore (Jealous husband).
- A skeleton wearing the light (Founder Red Light V3, 2.23).

**What's weak: a talking head with a listicle caption, or a plain unboxing.**
- 3 xmas gifts I bought my wife: 0.48.
- Top 3/5 stocking lists: 1.36–1.71.
- "Okay, first gift. For me?": 1.29.
- Comedy stage: 1.02.
- Military stock footage: 1.26.
- Animated songs: 1.36–1.72.

**Threat-to-the-product shots:** the product being cut, knifed or ground keeps showing up in winners (scissors, knife, grinder). It's a visual device worth testing.

**The overall rule:** the best hooks make **text and visual hit the same beat**. A drama label sits on a drama scene, and a number sits on a stack of that many gifts.

## Snow: a correction to "payoff first"
`05-11-2026 - [Snow] - [Nikita] - [PDP] - 2` ran at 2.22 ROAS and a **£23.17 CPA, the best of any gifting video**, with a 73% hook rate (last 7 days). The user rates this style highly (2026-11-07).

**What it does:**
- It **opens on the problem**: real-looking footage, filmed through a window, of a man clearing snow with a weak headlamp and falling into a snow pile.
- The verbal hook is the observer's live reaction: "This is why I hate him clearing snow in the dark. Oh god dammit!"
- The text hook is regret: "I should've bought him this sooner 😔".

**So the rule is:** frame one must be an **event**. Either a payoff (lamps lit, chaos) or a **comic fail with the weak old light**. What loses is a *static* problem, like Grown Men 4's sceptical talking heads, not problem-first as such.

**Why Snow drives comments (user, 2026-11-07):** a **son films his old dad** clearing snow in the dark and doesn't help. The dad falls, and commenters get **angry at the son**. The anger at the person filming is what gets people commenting, not just the fail. To reuse it: an older, sympathetic person struggles, someone young and comfortable films without helping, the fail is comic, and the older person ends up fine.
