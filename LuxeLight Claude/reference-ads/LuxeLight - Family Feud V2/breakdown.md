## 2. Six core elements

**Avatar.** The narrator is a gift-giver bringing presents to a big family Christmas. She brought five, for her brother-in-law, cousin, uncle, nephew and mom.
- The problem is unspoken. It's what to give a room full of relatives that isn't the usual candles and gift cards.
- The stake is social: being the one whose gift won Christmas. The hook says "chaos", and the close says it was the one everyone "actually fought over".
- She hasn't tried other solutions or failed with them. The only rejected alternatives are what everyone else brought: candles and gift cards.
- The buyer is a woman aged 65+, which matches who Meta spent on. But the narrator says "my mom uses hers", which puts her a generation younger than most buyers.

**Authority figure.** There's no expert. Proof comes from people:
- The family fighting over the gift.
- "The whole room went quiet" when the wave sensor was first used.
- The nephew in construction: "better than the $50 headlamp his company bought him". This is the strongest credibility beat. It's a working man's verdict, in passing, inside a family story.

The device is peer verdicts set inside a story, with no pitch voice.

**Mechanism of the problem.** Barely there. There's one line: "Not just one tiny spot like regular headlamps", backed by the LUXELIGHT vs OTHERS comparison at 27–31s. It's in plain language with no technical name, and only one layer deep. Nothing removes self-blame.

**Mechanism of the solution.** Feature-led with no build-up:
- The wave sensor: "Just wave your hand. Light turns on. Wave again. Off."
- The wide beam: "lights up everything at once".
- USB-C and the size: "small enough to fit in a stocking".

The brand name lands early, at 11.7s ("five of these LuxeLight headlamps"), which is 19% of the way through. No spec comes before the name, and rivals are only discredited generically ("regular headlamps").

**Level of awareness.** Unaware of the product, and aware of the problem only as a gifting problem. Proof: "Everyone else brought candles and gift cards. I brought five of these LuxeLight headlamps." It sells the gift occasion, not a lighting problem.

**Stage of sophistication.** Stage 5, identity and experience. Proof: "Best Christmas gift I've given that everyone actually fought over." The win is being the hero of the gift exchange. The features are the reasons it's believable.

## 3. Format and production variables

- **Hook (0–3.6s):**
  - One held AI-generated shot with no cuts. It shows a cosy Christmas living room with a fire and a tree. An older white man, around 70, with a white side parting, glasses, a brown tweed suit and a grey sweater vest, sits cross-legged in an armchair. He's reading a broadsheet newspaper with a lit headlamp on. Behind him, two younger men in Christmas jumpers wrestle on the floor and kick their legs in the air.
  - The device is calm in the foreground and chaos in the background, which is a visual open loop: why are they fighting?
  - Hook text: "THE $30 GIFT THAT NEARLY ENDED XMAS 😳" in black bold caps on a white box at the top centre, with "ENDED XMAS" in red.
  - 14 words of voiceover land in the first 3s, which is heavy lifting: the price, the conflict and the occasion.
- **Footage:**
  - From 3.6s it's real phone UGC, mostly upright handheld clips.
  - Mixed settings: a driveway at night, a living room with a tree, a garage, a utility room, a dark alley with a dog, a kitchen counter, a marble worktop.
  - Different people in different shots. Footage comes from the colour board, with pink and orange from 44s.
  - It's mostly lower-fi. Product shots are close-ups of hands.
- **Messenger:** a voiceover narrator only, probably female (unconfirmed). No one speaks on camera.
  - The family characters are each a different real person, matched to the line: brother-in-law, a tattooed man in his 20s on a driveway; cousin, a man in a suit by the tree; uncle, a man in a blue puffer and beanie; nephew, a blond man in his 20s in a garage; mom, a woman in a "LOVE PINK 1986" hoodie walking a greyhound.
  - The wave sensor demo is a grey-haired man in his 60s. The "genuinely excited" beat is a man in his 30s with a moustache, smiling.
- **Wardrobe:** the hook is formal and old-fashioned (tweed and sweater vest) against the young men's Christmas jumpers. Elsewhere it's everyday clothes.
- **Structure:**
  1. AI hook scene.
  2. Fast "who stole it" montage, 3.6–9.7s.
  3. Contrast with the other gifts.
  4. Brand reveal.
  5. Three feature stories, each tied to a family member: the room went quiet over the wave sensor, the nephew's wide beam, mom's hands-free dog walk.
  6. A quick-fire spec line.
  7. New colours.
  8. Free case.
  9. Offer.
  10. Callback close with an end card.
- **On-screen text:**
  - Captions are lowercase in a white box with black text, near the lower middle.
  - Colour coded: red boxes for the alternatives ("everyone else brought candles", "and gift cards"), green boxes for benefit lines (wide beam, "can see your whole work area", dog walk, USB-C, stocking, "genuinely excited"), and the brand in orange italics.
  - "LUXELIGHT" and "OTHERS" labels on the beam comparison.
  - The end card stacks "SHOP NOW FOR UP TO 50% OFF 🎁" in a white box, "60-DAY GUARANTEE ✅" in a white box and "LOW STOCK ⚠️" in a red box.
- **Audio and pacing:**
  - 270 words in 61.4s is 4.4 words a second, which is fast.
  - About 45 cuts gives a mean shot of about 1.3s. Cuts are tightest in the feud montage and the wave demo (0.3–0.6s shots at 16–22s), and slowest in the closing product shots (2–3s).
  - The single held hook shot is the longest shot in the ad.
- **Length:** 61s.

**Key questions and hypotheses:**
- **Hook scene.** *Why it might work:* a 65+ woman sees her own Christmas, with the calm grandad, rowdy sons and fireplace, before the product registers. *Research:* the same scene powers Grown Men Gifts, but there's no library yet to check further. *Hypothesis:* the "calm older man and chaotic family" Christmas scene beats a product-first opener on hook rate by 5+ points *(the threshold is a suggestion)* for the 65+ female buyer.
- **Gift-fight framing.** *Why it might work:* it borrows the rules of a gift-swap game the buyer already knows, so popularity is shown, not claimed. *Hypothesis:* gift-swap fighting as proof gets a lower CPA than a feature-first body for this avatar.
- **Narrator's age.** *Why it matters:* the buyer is 65+, but the narrator mentions "my mom". *Hypothesis:* a narrator speaking as the grandmother of the same family lowers CPA among the 65+ women Meta already chooses.
- **Offer position.** Only about 6% of 3-second viewers reach the offer at 54s. *Hypothesis:* moving "up to 50% off… how I grabbed five" to straight after the brand reveal (about 14s) lowers CPA without hurting AOV.

**Tier:** not assigned, because I don't have the break-even ROAS or CPA.
- On scale it's a strong winner: £92k on one creative, #5 out of the top 12 I read.
- On efficiency it's below the account: ROAS 1.74 against 1.87, and CPA £32.48 against £27.61. Its CPM of £71.60 is about 65% above the account's £43.36.
- It ranks lower than the main BOF control (2.11 ROAS at nearly 5× the spend).
- With a break-even ROAS I can place it on the F–S tier list.

**Things to check in the live ad:**
1. **The ad copy is UK-specific but runs in the USA/CA campaign.** It says "Fast UK shipping", "From £24.99" and "23,000+ people", while the video says "$30" and the site says "150,000+ customers". That copy is shared by all three Family Feud ads and the BOF Colour Apology ads in that campaign.
2. **"Just wave your hand. Light turns on."** In the product summary, the wave sensor only works after you click it on. "No fumbling with buttons" overstates this a little.
3. **"$30 gift"** is below the $36 single-unit price. It holds up only with the discount. That's low risk.

## 8. Reusable pattern and repurpose

**Pattern, as instructions:**
1. Open on one held AI-made scene of the buyer's own holiday: a calm older person using the lamp in the foreground and family chaos behind. Say the price and the conflict in the first 14 words.
2. Show popularity as a game everyone knows (stealing, stealing back, freezing) in four or five sub-1s cuts, one relative per line.
3. Name the dull alternatives in red, then reveal the brand with a number ("five").
4. Give each feature to a different family member with their own use: the room going quiet, the worker's verdict, mom's walks.
5. Put one comparison to an expensive rival in a peer's mouth ("better than the $50 one").
6. Stack the gift-fit specs (stocking size, USB-C), then the colours to match each person, then the free case as "gift ready".
7. Pay off the planted number in the offer line, and pay off the hook's conflict in the last line.

**Repurpose draft, for the Stitchers persona (the winning persona in CLAUDE.md). Lines are drafts to react to:**
- Hook scene: a sewing or quilting circle. A calm older woman stitches by lamplight in the foreground while two friends tug at a lit pink LuxeLight behind her. Text: "THE $30 GIFT THAT NEARLY ENDED QUILT NIGHT 😳"
- Montage: "Brenda takes it off Carol. Carol takes it back. Joan hides it in her sewing box."
- Alternatives: "Everyone else brought fat quarters and pincushions." **Gap:** check that this is real gifting language from Stitchers reviews or comments in Parker.
- Feature beats: hands-free light on dark fabric; "8 hours, so it lasts the whole session" (the battery claim the user approved for Stitchers); pick pink or orange to match each friend.
- Peer verdict: needs a real line from Stitchers reviews in Parker. Don't invent one.
- Close: "…which is how I grabbed five, one for everyone in the circle." / "Best gift our quilt group ever fought over."
