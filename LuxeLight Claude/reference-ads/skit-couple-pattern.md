## What the winners share
1. **The video opens mid-scene, with both people on screen bickering, and the product is already between them.** "Babe. You've done it again." "Walk the dogs, you never do it." "No, babe, no, no, no!" There's no setup and no one talking to camera alone.
2. **The conflict is small domestic stuff that every couple recognises:** who walks the dogs, a "recycled" gift, ranking gifts, "you're not having this one". It's never anything dark.
3. **There's a jealousy or ownership beat in the middle:** the aftershave "BY WHO!?", the pink one "No, you're not having this one, it's for the stables", "no more excuses… for me".
4. **The text hook teases the couple's fallout, often with a bracket:** (this didn't go well), (over this of all things), ARGUMENT INCOMING, Divorce papers are being drafted.
5. **Or it's an organic post format with the partner as the foil:** the honest ranking, or the mic interview "best gift I've ever gotten you".
6. **Features come up as conversation inside the argument,** and the use case is personal (dogs, garage, stables, fishing).
7. **The close turns into buying for others.** "Get one for your brother, get one for everyone", "I might just buy all of my family members one", plus the offer and the free case.

## What the losers share
- **A solo partner suspecting something** (Obsession, Who Is She: cheating, stalking, "worrying me"). These are one person talking to camera, with a mystery that pays off in a product, and nobody on screen to argue with. They got the worst hook and hold rates in the set, and ROAS as low as 0.03.
- **A fake price** ($700 Prank). The hooks were strong (64–71%), but ROAS was under 1. Anchoring on $700 likely clashes with the real price or confuses the buyer.
- **A slapstick visual with no couple conflict** (Headlight Skit) held attention but sold at break-even.
