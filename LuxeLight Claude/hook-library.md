## Rules, in the user's words
1. One short line, 3–8 words, with an emoji. No full stops and never two sentences ("I hate those"). Too long kills it, every time.
2. It must open a loop or spark curiosity, controversy, drama or comments. Don't give the answer away ("you're giving away the answer straight away").
3. Simple and snappy beats clever ("just something really simple… snappy and opens a loop"). Not too deep for a headlamp ("This is a headlamp. That's a bit too deep").
4. Build on what has worked in the account and what the user picked ("You need to use the script that's worked before"; "You were giving me good text hooks based off data… continue doing that"). Never write from scratch.
5. Take big swings: each hook in a set hits a different emotion, and each new round tries a different framework ("don't keep regurgitating the same framework"). Stay inside the ad's format, though: skit-couple lines don't suit story or talking-head ads.
6. It mustn't repeat the voice hook, contradict the body, or have nothing in the body to pay it off.
7. Match the script's emotion and stakes ("how does this person in this scenario want to feel?"). For story ads, take the hook from the story itself.
8. Single-persona ads: name the persona and open a loop in a few words. Broad family-gift stories: don't name the persona ("it just narrows the audience down"). Vary the callout ("don't keep saying 'blue-collar'").
9. Talking-head ads: the text is the speaker's own caption, in their voice. A blue-collar man is the authority, so he says "we" and "us" and talks about his years on the job.
10. Leading with the feeling or the problem has no loop. Lead with the win or the twist and hold back what the gift is.
11. Never call the buyer "broke", and don't mention socks or the hoodie in text hooks.
12. Bracket lines work when they add a payoff, a twist or credibility: (here's why), (wait for the end), (he was NOT happy), (I learned this the hard way), (do this instead), (20 years of gift-giving experience).
13. Caps are fine for some formats (THE GIFT THAT…, WIVES:, $700 GIFT PRANK…). Capitalise I, I'm and Christmas.
14. When asked for options, give plenty across different angles, with the body underneath. When asked for one hook, give one line. Keep it copy-paste clean, with no subheadings.

## Rejected, and why (patterns to avoid)
- Two sentences, or wordy mini-stories: "She's won Christmas 5 years running. Not this year 😏", "They fake it every Christmas, so this year I bought them all the same gift 😳".
- Plain callouts with no loop: "Crafters, look 👀", "Attention crafters 🧶", "Wives of tradesmen, SAVE THIS 📌".
- Statements of feeling or the problem: "Nobody tells you how much the fake thank yous hurt 💔", "Every year I said 'sorry, it's not much' 😔 (not this year)".
- Too deep or far-fetched: "The gift that finally made me feel like enough 🥹".
- Stale gift-guide lines: "GIFT IDEAS FOR MEN WHO HAVE EVERYTHING 🎁", "FOR THE MAN WHO "DOESN'T NEED ANYTHING" 🙄", "Is a headlamp a boring gift? 🤔".
- Unaware lines that don't qualify the viewer: "HIS DOT STOPPED MOVING FOR 20 MINUTES 😳".
- Repeating the voice hook: "...D LOST IT 😂 (5 stockings, under $90)".
- Lines that don't fit the script or are too niche: dog jump scares, "He sent his MOM out to the chickens in the dark 😡", crafter in-jokes like frogging and coal miner.
- Blue-collar rounds (Nov 21): "He said he wants nothing 🙄 (he's lying)", "PUT THE HOODIE BACK 🛑", "If his hands look like this, he needs #3 👀", "What a tradesman actually wants for Christmas 🛠️ (from a tradesman)", "Don't buy him ANYTHING until you see this 🛑", "Every blue-collar man wants this 👀 (he'll never admit it)", "I'd take this over a new drill 🔧 (no joke)", "My wife's best gift in 20 years 🎁 (and it was cheap)", "Buying for a blue-collar man (Difficulty: impossible) 😅". Reasons: "shocking", "regurgitating the same framework", "language wouldn't be suitable" (not in his voice), and "raw text hooks" written from scratch.
- Per-light prices in any hook ("ALL 3 COLORS UNDER $23 EACH"): "it looks deceptive".
- Object Naming Game V1 (Nov 21): "One of these is his Christmas gift 🤫 (he has no idea)", "10 years married 💍 (he failed every one)", "He pays ZERO attention to me 😭 (here's the proof)", "This quiz was a setup 🤫 (he fell for it)", "He thinks #3 finds my hair ties 💀". Reason: "you're giving the answer away. You're closing the loop straight away before it's even been opened." Don't state the result, the twist or his guess.
